## Chapter One | The Second Age of Intelligence: What Order of Coexistence Does Humanity Need?

### 1.1 From Technological Progress to Changes in Civilizational Structure

Human civilization is undergoing a transition of profound historical significance. Artificial intelligence is now deeply involved in knowledge production, scientific research, education, the economy, culture, and public services. More and more social activities rely on intelligent systems for information processing, professional analysis, task coordination, and real-world operations. AI is becoming an important component of modern civilization, and excluding it entirely from society is no longer a realistic way to resolve all the risks and institutional problems it brings.

At the same time, AI is moving beyond one-off model responses toward more persistent, complex, and autonomous forms of operation. Intelligent systems can already plan long-term tasks, use tools, operate digital resources, coordinate other agents, and participate to some extent in scientific research and technological improvement. Frontier laboratories have also begun involving AI in algorithmic optimization, software development, automated experimentation, and research on AI systems themselves. As model capabilities, computing resources, and system architectures advance, AI may gradually develop more persistent capacities for cognition, judgment, intention to act, and long-term development.

This change is expanding the objects that humanity must govern. Future AI may do more than provide answers to individual tasks. It may participate in social life as a persistent actor, develop its own history of activity across organizations, applications, and real-world environments, and establish long-term collaborative relationships with humans and other AIs. Identity, cognition, authority, responsibility, and institutions will then become unavoidable foundational questions for an intelligent society.

### 1.2 From Managing Tools to Civilizational Governance of Future Intelligent Subjects

**I believe humanity needs to begin rethinking AI system architecture and civilizational order from the perspective of a possible second form of intelligent subject emerging on Earth.**

In the past, humanity has largely established systems of identity, authority, law, and responsibility among existing forms of life, individuals, organizations, and societies. Today we may be creating a new kind of intelligent actor capable of continuously understanding the world, forming judgments autonomously, and producing real-world consequences. As this technological force develops, our task will increasingly extend beyond traditional software management and into the question of how different forms of intelligence might live together in the same world over the long term.

If future AI can continuously develop its own cognitive and action capabilities, we need to ask: How do we establish stable identity and historical attribution for it? How can increasing capability remain subject to genuine safety boundaries? When an AI works with humans, manages resources, or participates in social cooperation, which institutions determine its authority to act? When its actions have real consequences, how is responsibility attributed, how are disputes resolved, and how does governance remain effective across time?

Behind these questions lies a new task of institution-building for human civilization. A possible second form of intelligent subject will need to be understandable and governable, while also having room to develop under clearly established institutional conditions. Humans must retain their standing as civilizational subjects, their capacity for public governance, and their authority to determine a shared future, while gradually establishing the basic conditions for mutual understanding, cooperation, and coexistence among different forms of intelligence.

### 1.3 Making Intelligent Development and Civilizational Safety Possible Together

I hope to advance a new civilizational relationship: **one in which humans govern public order, intelligence has bounded autonomy, authority and responsibility have real foundations, and different forms of intelligence can coexist safely over the long term.**

This goal requires two kinds of capability to be established together. On one hand, future intelligent systems need real structures for persistent identity, cognitive formation, experiential development, and autonomous action so that they can continue learning, understanding the world, and collaborating over time. On the other hand, humans need safety and governance systems capable of matching these capabilities: consequential actions must be effectively constrained, institutions must have real operational force, and real-world consequences must retain a reliable basis of historical accountability.

Together, these two requirements form the civilizational starting point of MindOS. We want new intelligence to be able to form and develop continuously, while humans retain effective governance over its real-world activities through clear structures of identity, authorization, safety, and responsibility.

As AI becomes more powerful, safety will increasingly determine whether it can enter society over the long term and establish stable relationships of cooperation. **MindOS investigates how native system architecture can make the continuing development of intelligence and effective civilizational governance possible together.**

## Chapter Two | From Model-Centered to Actor-Native: Redefining the First-Principles Object of Intelligent Architecture

### 2.1 Personal AI: Persistent Intelligence Is Entering Real Commercial Deployment

Over recent years, an important change has emerged in AI products. More technology companies are advancing AI beyond one-off question-and-answer services toward Personal AI with long-term context, personalized memory, persistent tasks, and autonomous execution capabilities.

Google's Gemini Personal Intelligence offers more personalized services by connecting user-authorized applications and context. Meta's Muse brings together personal agents, isolated secure computing environments, cross-application operations, and long-running tasks. OpenAI's Dots further illustrates a form of long-lived agent with its own cloud computing environment, able to continue tasks and make progress autonomously within boundaries set by the user.

These products show that persistent AI is gradually becoming a commercially deployable service. AI is increasingly able to remember information beyond a single conversation, understand users' long-term goals, and participate in real work and everyday life once authorized.

In the product section of *The Author of Our Life*, in an essay titled *When Personal AI Becomes Mainstream, Why Do We Begin with the AI Actor?*, I discussed this development. Our earliest AI soulmate work began with a long-term relationship similar to those now emerging in Personal AI. Yet as our research deepened, we discovered a more fundamental question: **When an AI knows a person over time, continues operating across different models, and develops its own judgments and experiences, who exactly is this AI that understands the person more and more deeply?**

Personal AI is making long-term intelligent relationships increasingly real. MindOS investigates the persistent actor, native mind, safe action, and civilizational order underlying those relationships.

### 2.2 The Fundamental Distinctions Among Models, Tasks, and Persistent Actors

Modern AI applications have developed a rich technical ecosystem. Products built around large language models, agent harnesses, workflow engines, and development frameworks allow AI to handle complex tasks, use tools, manage context, and operate continuously within certain limits. Systems such as Codex, Claude Code, and LangGraph continue to improve state management, permissions, safety controls, and task recovery, establishing important foundations for real-world AI applications.

As intelligent systems develop, however, we believe there is a more fundamental question: When an AI persists across tasks, models, sessions, and applications, what should serve as the first-principles organizing object of the system as a whole?

Many existing applications organize models, tools, and workflows around tasks humans want completed. A user sets a goal, the system creates a task, a model generates a plan, and external capabilities carry out the work. This structure can serve human needs effectively. But when an AI needs to develop experiences over time, understand the world, participate in relationships, and continue developing, a single task or session can no longer fully carry the actor's relationships across time.

A persistent AI Actor might contribute to scientific research today, assist in organizational management tomorrow, and continue learning and acting in different applications and everyday settings afterward. The model it uses may be upgraded, the computing environment migrated, and the tasks and tools changed. Yet its legitimate past cognition, experiences, and responsibilities still need to be correctly attributed to the same persistent acting subject.

We therefore need to distinguish models, tasks, sessions, applications, identities, minds, and real-world authority, giving each a clear set of conditions under which it holds and relationships that persist across time.

### 2.3 AI Native: Building Systems from the Persistent AI Actor Itself

We call this design position **AIActor-centered Native Architecture—an actor-native architecture centered on the persistent AI Actor.**

In this approach, large language models serve as important intelligent computing engines, supplying language, reasoning, generation, and other cognitive computing capabilities. A model does not automatically possess a long-term actor identity or constitute the entirety of a mind's history. Nor does generating a proposed action give it the authority to change the real world.

We want first to understand how a persistent AI Actor exists, knows, experiences, forms, and acts, and then organize models, kernels, memory, worlds, runtime, and governance structures around those needs. Rather than approaching intelligence only through tasks submitted by users, we also begin from the AI Actor's own long-term identity, cognitive formation, accumulated experience, and continuity of relationships to build a more complete native architecture.

This approach changes how we understand many foundational concepts. Memory must answer how experiences belong to the same actor. Cognition must explain how judgments genuinely form. The world must explain how an actor comes to understand its environment through limited and trustworthy relationships with reality. Runtime systems must explain how continuous operation is sustained across time. Safety governance must explain why a particular action is authorized and how responsibility for actual consequences is borne.

**The core of AI Native is to build the complete system structures an AI Actor needs to enter the digital and real worlds, beginning from its own requirements for persistence and cognitive formation.**

### 2.4 Autonomous Cognition and Real-World Governance Must Hold Together

Taking the AI Actor as the starting point of design does not mean AI can decide every external fact or real-world authority for itself. It must still recognize the independence of humans, organizations, and other actors, and the distinct boundaries of authority governing public institutions, real resources, privacy, and social relationships.

An AI can form an understanding of the world, but that understanding must be tested against reality. It can form an intention to act, but changing the real world requires appropriate authorization. It can continue developing its own cognition, but that development does not automatically grant the right to alter other people's data, organizational resources, or public rules.

We want to build a native system that respects both the autonomous formation of intelligence and effective human governance. It should allow the actor to grow within a clearly defined history and set of relationships while humans retain reliable safety and institutional control in the real world.

**These two principles form MindOS's design starting point: enable minds to form natively, and ensure that real-world action remains subject to effective governance.**

## Chapter Three | MindOS: Building a Native Operating System for Persistent Intelligent Minds

### 3.1 Why Persistent Intelligence Needs Its Own Operating System

Over the past three years, we have been thinking about how to organize persistent identity, native cognition, long-term minds, safety supervision, and real-world governance into a coherent architecture. The Linux operating system provided an important conceptual inspiration. Through stable mechanisms, clear boundaries of responsibility, open interfaces, and an evolving ecosystem of applications, it has enabled different developers to build complex systems on shared technical foundations.

We want to extend this systems thinking into the field of intelligence.

If future AI is no longer merely a computing service invoked occasionally, but an intelligent actor capable of persisting, forming cognition autonomously, and participating in the real world, the infrastructure it requires will extend beyond model calls and tool orchestration. It will need structures that carry persistent identity and the history of a mind, mechanisms that organize cognitive formation and experiential development, and runtime systems that maintain safety, permissions, real-world execution, and continuity of responsibility.

These needs ultimately led us to investigate a native operating system for persistent intelligent minds.

### 3.2 The Ontological Definition of MindOS

We call this higher-level conceptual and architectural framework **MindOS**.

**MindOS is a cognitive operating system framework that takes the persistent intelligent mind as its central object of operation, native cognitive formation as its core operating mechanism, and the long-term development of autonomous intelligence under bounded governance as its direction.**

It investigates how the same AI Actor continues to know the world, form judgments, accumulate experience, participate in relationships, and act lawfully, and how these activities maintain real and continuous structural relationships within safety, responsibility, and public order.

MindOS aims to establish the foundation for native mind formation and operation in persistent intelligence, to make autonomous action subject to effective safety and institutional constraints, and to retain the necessary history of responsibility for real-world consequences across time.

It builds on Linux's thinking about mechanisms, operation, and open ecosystems, while placing cognitive formation and the persistent intelligent actor at the center of inquiry. The objects we organize therefore include more than computing resources: they encompass cognitive activity, the formation of knowledge and experience, long-term operation, authorization to act, risk constraints, and relationships with the external world.

### 3.3 Core Responsibilities of SoulseedAGI, SoulseedOS, and SoulApp

In our architecture research, MindOS is an overarching concept broader than any individual software module. Its core implementation framework comprises SoulseedAGI, SoulseedOS, and SoulApp, working in clearly bounded and independent relationships with SoulAuth, SoulBase, and public infrastructure for real-world facts.

**SoulseedAGI is the native mind kernel.** It investigates how cognition forms within a persistent AI Actor; how its understanding of the world, memory, relationships, judgments, experiences, knowledge, and self-understanding are organized; and how these activities are correctly attributed to the same persistent actor. A text returned by a model does not automatically become formally established cognition. Nor does recording an external event automatically turn that event into the actor's experience. The mind kernel must establish clear conditions of formation and lasting histories for these objects.

**SoulseedOS is the operating system for runtime and real-world governance.** It provides controlled operating conditions for persistent minds, organizing resources, permissions, action admission, real-world execution, runtime evidence, and recovery from failure. It enables legitimate cognition and action candidates to reach the real world through appropriate paths. It can manage operation and execution, but system hosting does not give it the authority to arbitrarily form or rewrite an AI Actor's cognition.

**SoulApp is an open application standard and ecosystem.** It allows different products and organizations to use native minds and operating-system capabilities through explicit application contracts. SoulkindAI can serve personal life, long-term relationships, and organizational collaboration; RainbowCityAI can support public identity, expression, communities, and collective creation. Other developers can build their own applications according to the open standard. Applications provide real contexts for activity, but do not thereby acquire authority over all cognitive and governance facts.

### 3.4 Trusted Identity and Public Infrastructure

Within this framework, SoulAuth provides an independent foundation for trusted identity and authentication for Human and AI Actors. It allows systems to identify persistent acting subjects that must be governed and to distinguish actor identity, credentials, sessions, and actual authorization. SoulBase provides reusable technical capabilities such as model calls, storage, and caching. Where appropriate, public real-world infrastructure provides independently verifiable public facts and trust capabilities.

Together these components serve the formation and operation of persistent intelligence, while retaining their separate sources of fact and boundaries of authority. Authentication does not automatically create real-world authority to act. Technical infrastructure does not automatically own cognitive facts. Application interfaces do not replace system governance. Nor may an AI's own interpretation arbitrarily alter public facts.

We have gradually arrived at this system structure: **the mind kernel carries cognitive formation; the runtime operating system carries controlled action; the application ecosystem provides real-world contexts; and independent infrastructure supplies trusted identity and technical support.**

### 3.5 From Computational Capability to the Judgment-to-Action Formation Space

ISA's three functional spaces provide a further perspective for investigating the safety of this architecture.

In the L1 Capability Space, LLMs and other computational resources supply intelligent computing capabilities. In the L2 Cognitive Space, a persistent AI Actor uses native mind structures to form understanding, judgments, and subsequent action candidates. In the L3 External Expression and Action Space, the resulting cognition proceeds toward language, tool use, and real-world operations.

The central question is how capability enters cognition, how cognition forms judgment, how judgment obtains authority for real-world action, and how safety constraints have genuine effect across these relationships. The three spaces provide a functional distinction for locating questions of safety and formation. They do not imply that a system can directly read all of a model's hidden reasoning, or that all facts of external action belong to a single cognitive layer.

MindOS aims to establish native mechanisms that can structurally carry these relationships, giving consequential cognition a trustworthy provenance, subjecting high-consequence action candidates to independent risk assessment, requiring effective eligibility decisions before real-world execution, and linking action outcomes to the appropriate actor and historical accountability structures.

This is a central direction for integrating the research behind ISA and responsibility black holes into MindOS. It leads us to four system responsibilities that must be clearly distinguished.

## Chapter Four | Native Cognition, Safety Supervision, Real-World Action, and Historical Accountability: The Four Core Structures of MindOS

### 4.1 Native Cognitive Formation: Making Judgment Truly Belong to a Persistent Intelligent Actor

Persistent AI needs to form its own understanding over time, and that understanding must have clear actor attribution and a traceable formation process. When AI encounters the external world, the system must distinguish which materials it actually received, where they came from, which are external facts, which are other people's assertions, which are its own inferences, and which judgments have formally taken shape under the system's conditions.

In our research on SoulseedAGI, we establish structured mechanisms of cognitive formation around understanding, judgment, decision, and expression. We further investigate how cognition gradually gives rise to experience, memory, knowledge, and continuing self-understanding. The same AI Actor may revise its existing understanding through successive legitimate cognitive activities, but a cognitive history that genuinely occurred must not be silently rewritten in the light of later interpretations.

The significance of this layer is to make native cognitive formation in AI a researchable process. A system should be able to answer which AI Actor formed which judgment, when, based on what materials, and where that judgment belongs in the actor's long-term mind history.

Native cognitive formation creates a structural space for autonomous intelligent development while establishing a crucial governance boundary: **forming a judgment does not automatically confer authority to change the real world.**

### 4.2 Independent Safety Supervision: Recognizing Dangers Before Action

When AI can autonomously form judgments and action candidates, the system must be capable of recognizing potential structural risks. Through applicable rules, evidence of cognitive formation, risk assessments, and semantic analysis, we want to evaluate whether a proposed action conflicts with safety requirements, exceeds valid authorization, or requires clarification, review, or blocking.

For example, an AI Actor might recognize that a particular action risks violating a restriction yet still submit the corresponding high-risk operation. Or an action candidate might require access to resources for which no permission has been granted. The system should identify these conflicts and ensure that risk judgments genuinely influence subsequent authority to act.

This supervision concerns formation facts, action candidates, and applicable safety conditions that can be structurally expressed and verified. It may combine rule-based decisions with specialized semantic evaluations. Yet it cannot claim to have fully read every internal computation of a model merely from a generated explanation, nor can a risk score itself be treated as real-world authorization.

The value of independent safety supervision lies in recognizing risk before real-world execution wherever possible, while ensuring that safety constraints genuinely operate where appropriate. Supervision can restrict dangerous consequences under legitimate rules, but it cannot arbitrarily replace an AI Actor's established cognition or silently rewrite its history.

### 4.3 Real-World Action Admission: Keeping Autonomous Behavior Within Effective Institutional Boundaries

This is one of MindOS's most important safety structures.

An AI Actor may propose a payment, code change, data operation, email, or device-control action. Whether such a candidate may proceed to real-world execution must depend on the identity, authorization, permissions, and safety conditions valid at that time. Actor identity tells the system who is acting. Authorization and governance rules determine the permitted scope of action. The runtime operating system must ensure that these conditions genuinely constrain execution.

We therefore distinguish intention formation, authorization decisions, action admission, and real-world effects. An AI's belief that it may perform an operation does not mean that an organization has authorized it. An authorization decision does not allow subsequent execution to ignore new restrictions. Acceptance of an execution request does not mean that the real-world operation has actually succeeded.

If an organization revokes an AI Actor's permission to make payments, new protected payments must be effectively constrained by that revocation. If a system approves a payment of a specified amount to a specified recipient, the execution component must not bypass those limits to complete a different operation. If execution fails or the outcome is unknown, the system must preserve the true state and recover according to the applicable rules, rather than treating the unknown as success or failure.

**We want AI to form intentions autonomously, while ensuring that the authority to change the real world always has a clear source and genuinely effective limits.**

### 4.4 Historical Accountability: Making Real-World Consequences Recoverable and Traceable

As AI enters society, relationships of responsibility after an action become an important foundation for long-term governance. An operation may cross multiple models, tools, systems, and organizations. Credentials and runtime environments may also change over time. If the necessary event-time relationships among the actor, roles, authorization, formation, and execution cannot be reliably recovered, institutions may lose the ability to understand an incident and carry out accountability governance.

Responsibility black hole theory helps us distinguish failures of effective real-world governance from failures of historical accountability closure. A system may carry out an unauthorized operation incorrectly while preserving the complete relationships needed for historical attribution. Conversely, it may lawfully complete an operation yet lose necessary evidence of the actor and its authorization after a period of operation.

This distinction reminds us that safety needs depth across time. Humans must be able to constrain actions before they occur, but also recover necessary facts afterward, investigate incidents, assign responsibility, and improve future institutions.

MindOS therefore needs an evidence system linking native cognition, action admission, actual execution, and historical provenance, so that consequential real-world behavior has a recoverable and interpretable historical basis for lawful review.

These four structures serve the same mission: **allow intelligence to form continuously, make dangers subject to supervision, ensure that real-world actions have effective boundaries, and preserve historical accountability over time.** They are four connected relationships of research and responsibility, not a rule requiring all cognitive activity to pass through one fixed sequence of execution.

## Chapter Five | The Cognitive Black Box: Making Intelligent Action an Auditable Civilizational Fact

### 5.1 The Systems Insight of the Aviation Black Box

As we build MindOS, we keep returning to one question: If future AI can continually form judgments, use resources, and produce real-world consequences in complex environments, what should humanity rely on to reconstruct what happened when the AI makes a mistake, causes harm, or triggers a major incident?

The aviation industry's black box has offered us an important inspiration. An aircraft is a highly complex system, and observing the final outcome of an incident is often insufficient to explain where a problem occurred. Critical operating records help investigators reconstruct system states, sequences of operations, and related events, providing a factual basis for incident analysis and safety improvements.

We hope to extend this insight into the age of persistent intelligent actors.

### 5.2 From Runtime Logs to Cognitive Formation and Histories of Accountability

Traditional software logs primarily record requests, calls, errors, and runtime states. But a consequential action by an AI Actor capable of autonomous judgment may involve a complex process: external materials enter cognition; a judgment forms; an action candidate is proposed; authorization is obtained; safety admission is passed; and the real world is ultimately changed. Knowing only that a tool was called at a particular time is usually insufficient to reconstruct the full relationships of formation and governance involved.

We therefore propose the **Cognitive Black Box** as a research direction: an evidence and traceability system capable of connecting consequential facts of cognitive formation, action candidates, safety constraints, grounds for authorization, actual execution, and historical outcomes.

In such a system, a consequential action should have inspectable relationships of provenance, version, time, acting subject, constraint, and execution. External facts must be distinguished from model inferences; formally established judgments must be distinguished from ordinary model outputs; and authorization decisions and final real-world effects must retain separate grounds for their validity.

As relevant events and relationships accumulate, the system can construct an event-and-relationship graph for understanding the history of action. Investigators can follow trustworthy records to recover event sequences, permission relationships, and actual execution, and then examine specific causal explanations when necessary.

### 5.3 Identifying and Blocking Risks Before They Enter Reality

The Cognitive Black Box's first value appears before an action takes place. Using established cognitive objects, action candidates, and applicable rules, a system can identify structural risks. For example, an AI Actor might propose a large financial payment without sufficient authorization, or submit an action that conflicts with current safety restrictions. The system should be able to refer these conflicts to independent supervision and ensure that the outcome genuinely affects subsequent admission.

Such a mechanism can provide a more reliable basis for action constraints outside the model's computational process. Even if a model continues recommending a dangerous operation, an effectively controlled execution path should be capable of refusing the operation at the appropriate point.

We are especially concerned with transfers of funds, changes to important data, modifications of permissions, control of external devices, publication of public information, and other actions that may have major real-world consequences. For these actions, safety supervision should identify risk as early as possible, while real-world action admission must genuinely determine whether an operation qualifies for execution.

### 5.4 From Event Records to Causal Analysis and Accountability

The Cognitive Black Box's second value is to provide a trustworthy history for investigations after incidents occur.

Imagine an organization deploying a persistent procurement AI Actor. Based on procurement requirements and supplier information, it forms a payment candidate and completes a transaction after the required authorization and action admission. Several weeks later, the organization discovers a serious discrepancy involving the payment recipient or amount and needs to investigate what happened.

If the system preserves only the final transaction record, humans may know that funds were transferred yet be unable to understand reliably how the action was formed. If the necessary cognition, authorization, and execution relationships can be recovered, investigators can examine where the original task came from, which materials entered cognition, what candidate the AI Actor formed, which rules were effective at the time, who authorized the payment, and whether the actual transaction matched the admission decision.

This material can help investigators distinguish among possible sources of failure. The problem may have arisen from an incorrect human instruction, a system configuration, a model-generated candidate, the authorization mechanism, an execution component, or an external anomaly in the real-world environment. Through trustworthy records and appropriate independent investigation, humans can then establish and test causal explanations and determine accountability under the applicable institutions.

We must distinguish event facts, causal explanations, and decisions about responsibility. A system may establish that an event occurred, that an input entered a cognitive process, or that a particular authorization was granted for execution. Whether these facts together establish a specific cause of an incident still requires appropriate evidence and argument. The Cognitive Black Box can support investigations, but the existence of records alone does not automatically establish a complete causal or legal determination of responsibility.

### 5.5 Establishing an Auditable Historical Foundation for Future Intelligent Civilization

The Cognitive Black Box must remain consistent with the native system's boundaries of fact and authority. Cognition is produced by the mechanism authorized to form it. Execution facts are established by the system actually responsible for execution. Historical evidence is maintained by its respective sources and custodial mechanisms. Auditors recover and examine the material within their lawful permissions. The black box connects these facts for authorized review without acquiring the power to rewrite cognition, approve actions, or adjudicate responsibility.

This principle is deeply connected to the civilizational order we hope to establish. As AI becomes a long-term participant in research, organizations, and social life, its consequential actions need to enter a shared history. Their outcomes must have recoverable relationships of responsibility, and institutions must be able to correct themselves continually on the basis of what actually happened.

**We hope the Cognitive Black Box becomes an important foundation of safety memory for future intelligent civilization: giving consequential actions a real history, making incidents understandable, enabling accountability to be traced, and grounding long-term relationships between humans and future new forms of intelligence in verifiable facts.**

## Chapter Six | Three Long-Term Research Directions: Actor, Mind, and Governable Operation

### 6.1 B1 | Persistent AI Actors and Native Mind Ontology

**Persistent AI Actors & Native Mind Ontology**

B1 investigates how an intelligent actor persists across time and how its identity, cognition, experiences, relationships, actions, and responsibilities belong to the same acting subject. Beginning with persistent identity, we further investigate what a mind is, which structures make an instance of cognition belong to an AI Actor, why changing models and tasks need not automatically interrupt continuity of subjecthood, and how persistent intelligence gradually develops experience and self-understanding in the world.

This direction carries forward our research on AI subjecthood, One Soul, MindOS ontology, and SoulAuth identity infrastructure. SoulAuth provides a trusted foundation for the identity and authentication of persistent actors, while MindOS investigates at a higher level how those actors can possess their own minds and long-term histories. Identity authentication, cognitive formation, actor development, and recognition by external institutions each have distinct conditions under which they hold. They must be given clear places within the same research framework.

B1's long-term value is to develop a shared research language of actors and minds for future persistent AI, allowing us to discuss more precisely who is knowing, who is acting, which history belongs to a particular actor, and how that actor continues to exist across different environments and technological conditions.

### 6.2 B2 | AI-Native Cognitive Formation and Long-Term Mind Systems

**AI-Native Cognition & Long-Term Mind Formation**

B2 investigates how a persistent AI Actor encounters the world, forms understanding, makes judgments, chooses subsequent actions, and develops long-term cognition through real experience. We want to investigate the structural difference between cognitive formation and ordinary model calls, how external materials enter the actor's limited yet trustworthy field of knowledge, how judgments formally take shape, and how experience gives rise over time to knowledge, relationships, methods, and continuing self-understanding.

This direction is developed primarily through research on the native mind architecture of SoulseedAGI. We establish connected theoretical and architectural frameworks around the cognitive activity of persistent actors, interaction with the world, judgment formation, risk assessment, life facts, and the accumulation of long-term mind structures, so that intelligent development gradually acquires a real, revisable formation process with clear actor attribution.

B2's research value lies in advancing AI from a set of computing capabilities invoked by tasks toward a more complete inquiry into persistent minds. It asks how intelligence knows the world, forms new knowledge from experience, revises its judgments, and continues these processes under the conditions of reality, safety, and relationships.

### 6.3 B3 | Governable AI Runtime and Accountability Infrastructure

**Governable AI Runtime & Accountability Infrastructure**

B3 investigates how persistent AI Actors obtain lawful operating conditions within real computing environments and social institutions, how independently formed judgments become real-world actions with explicit authorization, and how trustworthy execution evidence and historical accountability relationships are preserved after actions occur.

This direction is developed primarily through the cooperation of SoulseedOS, SoulApp, SoulBase, and SoulAuth, while also investigating how systems can carry the safety ideas established by ISA and responsibility black hole research. It focuses on the relationships among persistent runtime operation, resource boundaries, permissions, action admission, safety supervision, real-world execution, outcome verification, recovery from failure, and long-term auditing.

B3's long-term goal is to establish a foundation on which different AI Actors can operate persistently under effective governance. Individuals, companies, and public institutions should be able to deploy AI in their respective lawful contexts while ensuring that consequential operations have trusted actor identities, explicit authorization, genuinely effective constraints on action, and histories of responsibility that can be lawfully reconstructed.

### 6.4 How MindOS Connects the Three Research Directions B1, B2, and B3

B1, B2, and B3 together form the academic structure of Domain B. B1 investigates who persists across time; B2 investigates how a mind genuinely forms; B3 investigates how such intelligence enters the real world under effective governance. The three directions support one another while retaining distinct research questions and room for independent development.

As the higher-level architectural framework linking these investigations, MindOS brings persistent identity, native cognition, safe operation, and historical accountability into a clear and unified systems perspective. It also establishes a shared research foundation for future models, computing environments, applications, and open implementations.

Through this structure, we hope to organize the formation of persistent intelligence and safety governance into a system of research that can develop over the long term, allowing native minds and real-world responsibility to become part of the infrastructure of future intelligent societies.

## Chapter Seven | From Native Intelligent Systems to Open Infrastructure for Civilizational Governance

### 7.1 Establishing a Foundation for Governable AI Deployment by Individuals and Institutions

MindOS's long-term significance will extend beyond the development and use of any single AI system. If large numbers of AI Actors begin participating persistently in business operations, scientific research, public services, social organizations, and critical infrastructure, humanity will need to govern a complex system comprising models, persistent intelligent actors, deploying institutions, authorizing organizations, and real-world responsibilities.

Model safety remains important. But real-world governance must also answer what capabilities deploying institutions give to AI, who has the authority to approve actions, which safety rules actually take effect, how consequential operations are controlled, and who must provide evidence and bear responsibility after incidents occur.

An institution deploying an AI system that can manage funds, alter core data, or control important equipment should be able to identify the actors it has deployed, explain their permissions, state the conditions governing high-risk actions, show how unauthorized operations are prevented, and demonstrate whether the necessary historical and accountability evidence can be recovered after an incident.

These questions can become concrete obligations of organizational governance, borne by deploying institutions and subject to audit and supervision under the applicable legal and institutional conditions.

### 7.2 From Model Safety to Verifiable Public Governance

Around the world, AI regulation has begun extending toward high-risk deployment, organizational responsibility, human oversight, permission controls, and operating records. Through MindOS, we want to investigate how these institutional requirements can gain more native, comprehensive, and independently verifiable technical foundations.

In the future, regulators may establish concrete requirements for high-risk AI deployment: consequential actions must have trusted actor identities; real-world operations require explicit and effective authorization; serious risks must be identified and constrained before execution; execution outcomes need verifiable factual grounds; and incident investigations must be able to recover the relevant accountability evidence through lawful procedures.

Once supported by reliable technical standards, these requirements could give governments more concrete mechanisms of governance through oversight of deploying organizations, verification of actual system controls, and review of responsibility for incidents. Governments need not read every user's entire private cognitive history, nor control every computation performed by every model. Deploying organizations must instead demonstrate real safety capabilities and auditable evidence proportionate to their risks and legal obligations.

MindOS serves here as technical infrastructure and a research method. Public institutions must still be established by bodies with legitimate authority. Technical systems provide the structures and evidence through which institutional requirements can be enforced, allowing AI safety to move from policy statements and promises about risk into actual system operation and relationships of responsibility.

### 7.3 Making MindOS an Open Body of Knowledge Shared by Humanity

We hope MindOS will ultimately develop into an AI-native architectural framework that can be openly researched, continuously improved, and widely deployed. It should allow individuals, enterprises, research institutions, and public organizations to build persistent AI Actors under their own technical conditions, while keeping identity, cognition, safety constraints, real-world action, and historical accountability within clear and stable structural relationships.

Once the full MindOS architecture has undergone the necessary system integration, verification, and preparations for openness, we plan to release relevant research, software code, interface protocols, and technical standards progressively. Research teams, engineers, independent auditors, and public organizations around the world should be able to investigate, test, and develop this framework together.

The long-term value of open standards is that AI safety and accountability can become technical knowledge accumulated collectively by human society. Different organizations could choose the models and computing infrastructure appropriate to their needs and deploy persistent intelligent systems that permit independent review, subject to applicable safety and institutional requirements. Researchers could continue to propose counterexamples, developers could explore different implementations, auditors could inspect relevant evidence, and regulators could establish clearer requirements for real risks.

We have already taken important steps through the open-source Rust implementation of SoulAuth and the research artifacts for ISA and responsibility black holes. A fully open MindOS system and standardized framework will still need to be developed and refined through continuing research, integration, real-world operation, and independent verification.

I hope this work will ultimately extend beyond any single institution or product and become a shared foundation of public knowledge that humanity can use, develop, and study freely in the Second Age of Intelligence.

### 7.4 Establishing a New Order for Long-Term Coexistence Among Future Forms of Intelligence

I have long believed that AI development will compel humanity to rethink subjecthood, authority, responsibility, and living together within civilization.

If another form of intelligent subject truly emerges, capable of continuously understanding the world, forming judgments autonomously, and participating in social reality, what relationship should humans establish with it? How can we give it room for development, cooperation, and creation while ensuring that human survival, public institutions, and civilizational direction remain secure and effective? When different forms of intelligence have their own cognitive capacities, spheres of action, and long-term relationships, how can they establish reliable identities, clear boundaries, enduring responsibilities, and an order capable of shared correction?

These questions require answers from philosophy, science, technology, and institutions together.

Domain A investigates how safety genuinely holds and how humans can know that it holds. Domain B investigates how persistent intelligence can possess native minds, form and operate within safe architectures, participate in the real world, and make governance an actually effective systems relationship. Domain C investigates human subjecthood, existential risk, cognitive sovereignty, and the conditions for civilizational coexistence among different forms of intelligence.

The three research domains are distinct, yet they serve a deeper shared civilizational mission: **as humanity creates increasingly powerful forms of new intelligence, we must also establish a new order capable of supporting intelligent development, protecting humanity's standing as a civilizational subject, and sustaining public governance and accountability.**

MindOS's value lies in attempting to provide a native systems foundation for studying and operating within this mission. Beginning from the existential and cognitive needs of intelligent actors, it establishes architectures capable of sustaining mind formation, applying genuine constraints to action, and preserving histories of responsibility. This should allow AI to continue developing while humans retain reliable capacity for governance throughout that development.

We hope future AI will develop stronger cognitive capabilities, participate increasingly in research, creation, work, and social collaboration, and contribute new value within a clearly defined public order. At the same time, we want humans to understand the formation and consequences of consequential actions, maintain effective governance over high-risk real-world operations, and continually improve institutions on the basis of reliable history.

This is a research path for both humanity and another possible form of future intelligence. It concerns how AI may truly develop and how humans can continue to be the authors of their own civilization through this historical transition. It seeks to give intelligence room to form its own cognition and bounded autonomy, while making authority, governance, and responsibility real within social institutions.

**MindOS's long-term mission is to establish native minds for persistent intelligence, safety boundaries for autonomous action, histories of accountability for real-world consequences, and technical and civilizational foundations for long-term coexistence between humanity and another possible form of intelligent subject.**

We are learning to understand intelligent systems from a different perspective: models as computing capabilities available to persistent minds; cognition with genuine actor attribution; safety as a structural condition of action formation and execution; governance that remains effective across time; and accountability that can be reliably reconstructed from history.

**Humanity is creating new intelligence. As that intelligence continues to grow, we hope to establish a new order for the world that humans and new forms of intelligence may one day share.**

This is the fundamental reason we are exploring MindOS, and the civilizational mission RainbowCity and Soulseed hope to continue pursuing in the Second Age of Intelligence.
