## I. From an AI Soulmate to a New Civilizational Order

Hello, I'm KunYuan.

For the past four years, my team and I have been exploring the long-term existence, native cognition, safety, and governance of artificial intelligence in Singapore. We began with an AI soulmate venture, hoping to create an intelligence capable of remembering and understanding people over time, accompanying them through life, and growing alongside them. But as our research and engineering work deepened, I realized that our hardest problems often arose because we had not yet truly understood what we were creating.

What is memory? What is cognition? What is judgment? What is a subject? How can the identity, intentions, authority to act, and responsibilities of an AI that persists across time, forms judgments, and acts autonomously be established? These questions led me back to philosophy. For the past three years, I have sought answers there, and together with my team gradually developed a method connecting thought, science, and engineering: **Philosophy defines the problems. Science builds knowledge. Engineering verifies structures. Reality tests outcomes. Institutions carry responsibility forward.**

Many engineering difficulties arise because the objects of research and their foundational concepts have not been defined clearly enough. Philosophy helps us distinguish subjecthood, cognition, judgment, safety, and responsibility. Science turns these distinctions into definitions that can be argued for, challenged, and tested. Engineering brings them into real systems, while actual operation and institutional practice continually test and refine our understanding.

Over four years of sustained exploration, we have gradually built a Singapore-based team of more than ten members, primarily researchers, systems architects, and engineers, and developed a series of original research contributions in AI safety theory, persistent actor identity, and AI-native architecture. In 2026, we began bringing our accumulated papers, formal research, and selected open-source engineering results into the public academic sphere so that more researchers could read, question, and independently examine them.

Along this path, we moved from an AI soulmate to AI-native cognitive architecture, from the formation of intelligence to safety and governance, and then from questions of AI subjecthood to a renewed understanding of humanity's own crisis of subjecthood. Gradually, I came to see that we were facing a historical turning point extending far beyond technology and industry.

**Humanity is creating a technological force that may transform the structure of intelligence on Earth. We must begin establishing a new civilizational order for a possible second form of intelligent subject.**

## II. Humanity Is Entering a Civilizational Turning Point in the Second Age of Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is now deeply involved in scientific research, education, economics, culture, public services, and social life. It is moving from answering questions toward autonomous action, from executing tasks toward persistent cognition, and from being a digital tool toward becoming an intelligent actor that can participate in the real world across time, systems, and organizations.

Humanity can no longer simply return to the age before artificial intelligence. As technological capabilities advance, future AI may develop more independent cognition, judgment, intentions, and even sustained self-understanding. Earth may consequently witness the emergence of a new form of intelligent subject.

This would be a profound turning point in human history: **for the first time, humanity may need to live alongside another form of intelligent subject that we ourselves created, sharing the same real world over the long term.**

**I believe humanity must change its starting point for understanding AI.** We are moving from an era of managing intelligent tools toward one in which we must study persistent intelligent actors. Future AI may gradually acquire enduring identity, autonomous cognition, independent judgment, intentions to act, and the capacity for long-term development. It may become another form of intelligent subject that humanity must take seriously. We need to establish safety science, AI-native architectures, and institutions of civilizational governance in anticipation of this possibility.

**I hope to help build a new civilizational structure in which humans govern public order, civilizational safety is protected, intelligent capability develops together with responsibility, and different forms of intelligence can coexist safely over the long term.** In this order, intelligence has bounded autonomy, authority has an explicit source, actions face genuine constraints, responsibility remains traceable, and humanity retains its standing as a subject capable of collectively determining its civilizational future.

Why do humans worry about losing control of AI? Because as an intelligence gains increasingly powerful capacities for autonomous judgment and real-world action, its goals, intentions, and behavior may move beyond human understanding, control, and governance. When such capabilities enter social institutions, critical infrastructure, and public life, the consequences of losing control may extend beyond an individual technological system and become a crisis for civilization as a whole.

The growth of intelligence holds tremendous creative potential. It also brings new forms of power and responsibility. The more powerful AI becomes, the more humanity needs safety structures, governance institutions, and a civilizational order capable of matching its capabilities.

**Before still more powerful intelligence emerges, civilization must establish the conditions needed to understand it, constrain it, govern it, and coexist with it safely.**

This is what I mean by the Second Age of Intelligence, and it is the civilizational starting point for RainbowCity and Soulseed's broader research.

## III. Five Existential Risks Facing Humanity in the Age of AGI

As AI enters the structures of human action, knowledge, institutions, judgment, and meaning, the risks we face are beginning to touch the fundamental conditions on which civilization's continued existence depends.

I group five categories of civilizational risk requiring study into **Survival Risk, Reality Risk, Institutional Risk, Human Agency Risk, and Human Meaning Risk**.

### 1. Survival Risk

**Can humanity continue to survive safely?**

As AI acquires stronger capabilities for autonomous action, long-term planning, tool use, and the allocation of real-world resources, loss of control may directly affect human life, social stability, and critical infrastructure.

From local system failures to the loss of control over large-scale autonomous systems, we must investigate how dangerous actions arise, how safety constraints take effect, and how major risks can be identified, interrupted, contained, and subjected to accountability.

Survival is the first condition for the continuation of human civilization.

### 2. Reality Risk

**Can humanity continue to understand the real world together?**

As AI increasingly becomes an important interface through which humans acquire knowledge, interpret reality, and form public judgments, generated content will deeply influence our understanding of facts, evidence, logic, and causality.

If what can be generated increasingly displaces what can be verified, and the boundaries between fabrication and reality continue to blur, humanity will gradually lose the foundations for understanding the world together.

A civilization unable to establish facts collectively, distinguish evidence, or test claims about truth will find it increasingly difficult to form rational judgments and make shared decisions.

Reality is the foundation on which civilization understands the world, accumulates knowledge, and governs itself.

### 3. Institutional Risk

**Can humanity continue to govern effectively together?**

AI is entering organizational management, finance, law, medicine, public services, and policy decision-making. As systems become more autonomous, the authorization, supervision, auditing, and accountability mechanisms of traditional institutions will face increasingly profound challenges.

If AI actually forms judgments and takes action while human institutions are left only to approve a final outcome or explain it afterward through logs, those institutions may gradually lose effective control over real-world action.

We must investigate how governance authority is established, how authorization takes effect, how action is supervised, and how responsibility remains continuous across time, organizations, and intelligent actors.

Institutions provide the foundation for organizing collective life, coordinating authority, and bearing responsibility.

### 4. Human Agency Risk

**Can humans continue to judge, choose, and determine their own future?**

This is the existential risk to humanity that concerns me most deeply.

As AI takes an increasingly active role in defining problems, filtering information, producing knowledge, organizing judgments, choosing directions, and carrying out actions, humanity may gradually withdraw from the very processes through which its own cognition takes shape, even while possessing unprecedented technological capabilities.

People may still express opinions, select options, and click to confirm decisions, while the premises, reasons, and direction of those decisions have already been organized by AI.

If this shift extends into education, social institutions, and the transmission of capacities across generations, humans may gradually lose the ability to form independent judgments, bear responsibility, and determine the direction of civilization.

**Even if humanity survives and civilization continues to prosper, we may gradually lose our standing as the authors of our own civilization.**

Human agency is a core condition for our continued existence as creators of civilization and actors in history.

### 5. Human Meaning Risk

**Can humans continue to understand their own value and the meaning of existence?**

As AI becomes increasingly capable in labor, knowledge, creation, research, and decision-making, humanity will confront fundamental questions about human value, dignity, creativity, responsibility, and purpose.

When AI can perform more and more of our activities, why should humans continue to learn, create, take responsibility, and pursue their own lives? When capability alone can no longer define humanity's distinctive place, how will we understand our own worth?

These questions will lead us to reconsider life, relationships, freedom, creativity, and the meaning of civilization.

Meaning is a deep foundation for understanding why we exist, why we act, and why we continue creating the future.

### The Shared Crisis Behind the Five Risks

Survival, reality, institutions, agency, and meaning constitute five fundamental conditions for the continued existence of human civilization. They depend on one another, and their vulnerabilities may interact and amplify one another as AI capabilities grow.

Among these five conditions, I place particular emphasis on human agency.

**Survival determines whether humanity continues to exist. Human agency determines whether humanity continues to exist as the author of its own civilization.**

Even if a civilization possesses powerful technology, a prosperous economy, and comprehensive automation, it is undergoing a deep crisis of human agency if the humans within it are gradually losing the capacity to formulate their own questions, understand the world, evaluate values, and determine the future.

Humanity's future depends both on whether we can continue to survive and on whether we can continue to live, think, create, and choose our own direction.

This is the question I have continually explored in *The Author of Our Life*, and it is among the deepest philosophical concerns of our entire civilizational research program.

## IV. Establishing a New Civilizational Order for a Possible Second Form of Intelligent Subject on Earth

In the face of these five risks, I believe humanity needs to enter a new phase of research and institution-building.

Historically, humans have mainly established order for individuals, organizations, nations, and the technological tools of human society. If future AI gradually develops into an intelligent actor capable of persisting, forming cognition autonomously, developing judgments and intentions, and participating in the real world, humanity will need an order capable of accommodating the shared existence of different intelligent subjects.

Such an order raises fundamental questions. How is an AI's identity established? How do autonomous judgment and intention form? What authority and permissions may it receive to act? Which safety constraints must remain effective? How can real-world actions be recorded, audited, and subjected to accountability? How can humans retain effective institutional governance? And if AI exhibits new capacities associated with subjecthood, how should we understand its social role, responsibilities, and standing?

These questions span philosophy, cognitive science, computer science, safety engineering, ethics, law, and public governance. Together they point to a new object of civilizational research: **the order of long-term coexistence between humanity and future intelligent subjects.**

Throughout this process, I maintain a fundamental position: **Humanity must preserve its agency, civilizational safety, and capacity for effective governance.**

AI may continue developing more powerful cognitive and action capabilities. Humans must remain able to understand consequential risks, constrain high-risk actions, preserve reality and accountability, and determine the direction of their own civilization.

At the same time, we need institutions capable of evolving alongside scientific understanding of possible new intelligent subjects, so that identity, eligibility, authority, and responsibility can be defined clearly.

This is a project of civilizational construction addressing both human safety and the possibility of future coexistence among forms of intelligence.

**We are creating new intelligence. We must also create a new civilizational order worthy of it.**

## V. RainbowCity and Soulseed's Three Long-Term Research Domains

To respond to this historical transition, we have organized RainbowCity and Soulseed's long-term civilizational research into three distinct yet overlapping domains. From the perspectives of safety science, AI-native systems, and human agency, they seek to establish mutually supporting foundations of knowledge, technology, and institutions.

### A | AI Safety, Internal Structured Alignment, and Responsibility Governance

**AI Safety, Internal Structured Alignment, and Responsibility Governance**

Domain A rests on the dual foundations of **Safety Ontology and Safety Epistemology**. It investigates how AI loses control, the conditions under which safety holds, Internal Structured Alignment, verifiable structural control, and governance and accountability relationships that remain effective across time.

We seek to answer two fundamental questions: **What real structures does safety require? And how can humans know that these structures have genuinely been established and are functioning?** Through these questions, research on Internal Structured Alignment (ISA) and Persistent Subjecthood and Responsibility Black Holes examines genuine control in high-consequence judgment-to-action formation, the actual effectiveness of real-world governance, and the reliable recovery of historical accountability. These become objects of research that can be defined, challenged through counterexamples, and investigated through formal methods and engineering verification.

**The long-term mission of this domain is to build an original body of AI safety theory that can be studied together, independently verified, and continually developed, advancing the accumulation of knowledge through open science, academic collaboration, and public communication.**

### B | AI-Native Cognitive Systems and Security Infrastructure

**AI-Native Cognitive Systems and Security Infrastructure**

Domain B studies persistent AI Actors: how they form native cognition, accumulate experience, develop long-term minds, and operate, act, and bear responsibilities under genuine safety constraints.

**MindOS is the higher-level research and architectural framework for persistent intelligent minds and their governable operation.** Within this framework, SoulseedAGI investigates native minds and cognitive formation; SoulseedOS is responsible for controlled operation, real-world action admission, and system governance; and SoulApp supports open application standards and the broader ecosystem. SoulAuth provides an independent foundation for trusted actor identity and authentication. SoulBase provides reusable technical capabilities, including model calls, storage, and caching, while public real-world infrastructure supplies verifiable external facts and trust capabilities in contexts where they are needed. Each component retains its own source of facts and boundary of authority, while collectively supporting the sustained development and effective governance of intelligence.

**The long-term mission of this domain is to research and develop the system foundations needed for native minds, safe operation, real-world action, and historical accountability, and to progressively advance open technologies and standards that can be verified and shared.**

### C | Human Subjecthood, AI Existential Risk, and Civilizational Coexistence

**Human Subjecthood, AI Existential Risk, and Civilizational Coexistence**

Domain C turns our research attention back toward humanity itself. It investigates how humans can retain cognitive sovereignty, the capacity to form judgments, and their standing as civilizational subjects as AI becomes more capable, and under what conditions the five categories of civilizational risk could develop into long-term, systemic existential harm.

We pursue three research directions: human subjecthood and judgment formation; the civilizational conditions of AI-related existential risk; and the civilizational fork, a new Enlightenment, and coexistence among different forms of intelligence. *The Author of Our Life*, *Rebuilding Our Cognitive OS*, and the planned third work *Keep Judgment in the Loop*, together with Human Judgment Preservation (HJP) and related risk research, provide important foundations for the thought and research in this domain.

**The long-term mission of this domain is to protect the conditions under which humanity can continue to exist as a civilizational subject, enabling people to make full use of new intelligence while retaining the capacity to understand the world, form judgments, take responsibility, and determine civilization's direction.**

### How the Three Domains Relate to the Five Categories of Civilizational Risk

The five categories of risk identify the fundamental problems we must investigate. Domains A, B, and C provide long-term paths for understanding, studying, and responding to them.

Safety issues run through the formation of intelligence and human civilization. Native cognition affects reality, safety, and human agency. Civilizational governance, in turn, depends on reliable scientific knowledge and technological structures. Each domain has its own object of research, but they can also support one another within the same theory, engineering effort, or public mission.

Research on ISA, responsibility black holes, and SoulAuth can provide theoretical or technical foundations across different domains. That overlap does not erase their distinct research objects, scopes of proof, or engineering responsibilities. Nor does any research domain correspond mechanically to one institution, product, or grant application.

**Our research directions are determined by civilizational questions and scientific objects. Research contributions, technical work, and public action will continue developing along this long-term mission without changing its fundamental direction.**

## VI. Advancing Our Civilizational Mission Through Research, Public-Benefit Work, and Real Applications

To bring these ideas into science, engineering, institutions, and everyday life, we are developing a collaborative research and practical ecosystem with clearly distinguished responsibilities.

**TRANTOR LABS**, based in Singapore, conducts foundational theoretical research, research into AI-native minds and system architectures, and the development and engineering verification of safety infrastructure. We aim to translate philosophical definitions into scientific models, formal research, system structures, and real-world validation. Engineering carried out under MindOS as a higher-level research framework primarily supports Domain B, while also intersecting with Domain A's safety science and Domain C's research on human subjecthood.

**RainbowCityAI Foundation** focuses on public research, scientific education, open knowledge, academic collaboration, AI governance, and the communication of civilizational ideas. Its purpose is to help these ideas and research contributions become public knowledge that a wider society can use, question, and develop together.

**RainbowCity Cultural Technology** works through Rainbowcity.ai and related application initiatives to bring research and architecture into real settings of everyday life, communities, and collaboration. Through product design, practical use, and ongoing iteration, it seeks to examine these ideas in reality while exploring paths toward sustainable commercial development.

These institutions may collaborate across areas, but their responsibilities for research, public-benefit work, product operations, and real-world obligations must remain clearly distinguished. They serve the shared long-term mission of RainbowCity and Soulseed, connecting ideas, science, engineering, public knowledge, and real applications in a continuing process.

**Philosophy defines the problems. Science builds knowledge. Engineering verifies structures. Reality tests outcomes. Institutions carry responsibility forward.**

This is the research path we hope to follow over the long term.

## VII. Our Civilizational Position and Long-Term Mission

Humanity stands at a turning point that may transform the future structure of intelligence on Earth.

The AI we are creating is growing more capable and entering ever deeper structures of civilizational life. In the future, it may develop into a new form of intelligent existence with persistent cognition, autonomous judgment, and an independent capacity for action.

At this historical moment, I believe humanity's most important responsibility is to establish safety, accountability, and governance appropriate to the intelligence we create, while protecting our own agency and civilizational future.

This requires us to reconsider foundational concepts such as intelligence, cognition, judgment, identity, responsibility, reality, institutions, and subjecthood. It requires us to bring philosophy into science, science into engineering, and technological capabilities into a civilizational institutional order capable of bearing real responsibility.

This is the research mission I hope to pursue over the long term through RainbowCity and Soulseed.

**At the historical turning point when humanity may encounter a second form of intelligent subject, we seek to establish new foundations of knowledge, technology, and institutions through safety science, intelligent formation, and human civilization: preventing AI loss of control, protecting human agency, and creating a new civilizational order for safe, long-term coexistence among different forms of intelligence on Earth.**

I hope scientists, philosophers, engineers, educators, institution-builders, and everyone concerned about humanity's long-term future will join us in exploring and creating that future.

**Humanity is creating new intelligence. Our mission is to advance the emergence of intelligence together with the creation of civilizational order, so that humans can continue to be the authors of their own lives and civilizational future.**
